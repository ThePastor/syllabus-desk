About this project

what it is · how to use it
The problem

The dates exist. Nobody can see them.

Every course hands you a PDF with the schedule buried in a table on page four, in a different layout from the last one. Five courses means five documents, five formats, and no single place that says what is due this week.

So most people read the outline once in September and never again — and find out about the midterm when someone mentions it in the hallway.

Worse: the outline is not the whole story. Instructors post quizzes, lessons and gated activities on the course site that the outline never mentions.

What this does

One pass, then it is just there.

You add each course once. Claude reads the outline and proposes the dated items — and then you confirm every one before anything is saved. Nothing is guessed: if the outline never gave a date, it comes back flagged, not invented.

After that you get your term three ways: this week and next for what is actually coming, a full-term calendar for the shape of it, and course by course for the weightings and the rules that quietly cost marks.

Then export it, so the reminders reach you when you are not looking at this page.

Step 01

Add a course

Press Add course. Drop in the PDF or Word file your instructor posted, or paste the text straight from it. The file is read on your own device — it is not uploaded anywhere.

If you only paste part of it, paste the schedule table and the grading breakdown. Those two carry almost every date.

Step 02

Check what it found

Everything lands in an editable table before it is saved. Fix a title, correct a date, change a weight, delete a row that is not really graded.

Rows marked no date are the ones the outline left blank — usually final exams. Fill them in when your school publishes them, or leave them flagged so you remember they are open.

Step 03

Do the rest, then set your study blocks

Repeat for each course. On the Courses tab you can add as many weekly study blocks per course as you want, move them to whatever evenings suit you, and remove the ones you do not keep. Each course also suggests times worked out from when it actually meets, avoiding your other classes and blocks — and extra prep is added automatically five, three and one days before every exam.

Step 04

Send it to your phone

On Export, take the .csv for Google Calendar or the .ics for Apple Calendar and everything else. Import it into a separate calendar, not your main one — if it comes out wrong you delete one calendar instead of forty events.

The export is a snapshot. Change a date here afterwards and your calendar will not know, so re-export or fix that one event by hand.

On your phone

Keep it on your home screen

Added to your home screen it opens straight into your term with no address bar, and looks like any other app. It takes about ten seconds and installs nothing.

It still needs a connection to open — there is no offline copy. On iPhone the home-screen version keeps its own storage separately from Safari, so use Export data and import it once inside.

What it will not do

  • It will sometimes get a date wrong. It reads outlines with an AI model, and outlines are messy. This is a planning aid, not a source of truth — your course page and your instructor are. Check anything that matters.
  • It only knows what you give it. If your instructor posts a quiz on the course site that never appears in the outline, it will not be here. Worth a look at your course page every couple of weeks.
  • Without an account, your term is saved in this browser only. Not on a server, not visible to anyone else — but also not on your phone, and gone if you clear site data. Export data on the Export tab is your backup. With an account, your email and your term are stored on a server run by JohnsonXCorp so they can follow you between devices, and your email is visible to the site owner. Your password is not — only a one-way hash of it is kept, which nobody can read back. Accounts are optional and the Account tab can delete what is stored.
  • Reading an outline sends its text to Claude. That one step, and only that step, leaves your device. Everything else — the file itself, your schedule, your edits — stays local.

How this page is built

One source, two outputs, so they cannot drift: body.html plus build.py writes the artifact body and the standalone index.html together. The JohnsonXCorp mark is injected from jxc-logo.svg at build time and the build fails if it is missing. Never edit the outputs directly.

Design tokens are the UNI Made EZ system: teal #0E7C86, lime #D9F56B, coral #FF7A59, Inter Tight over Inter with JetBrains Mono, 2px corners, inverted footer.

What this sign-in is and is not

The username and password are stored as a SHA-256 hash in OWNER_HASH, so the password itself is not sitting in the source in readable form. That is better than plain text, and it is still not security: the check runs in your browser, on a page anyone can read and edit, so a determined person gets past it. Treat it as a lock on a glass door.

Which means: treat everything shipped in this file — including the schedule behind Load my schedule — as public, because on a public web address it is.

To change the credentials: compute sha256("username:password"), replace OWNER_HASH in src/body.html, rebuild and republish.

If you want a schedule that is genuinely private, keep it out of the build and bring it in through Import on the Export tab from a JSON file you hold yourself.

Across a programme

for whoever schedules them

What this is

A department reads one outline at a time. A student reads all of them at once, and that is where the trouble is: two outlines can each be faultless on their own and still put a midterm and a term paper on the same Tuesday.

Paste the outlines a cohort takes together — or drop in the course files from the checker — and this puts them on one timeline: every day carrying two courses, every overloaded week, every deadline on a statutory holiday.

It works entirely on this device. Nothing is uploaded, nothing is stored, and closing the tab is all the cleaning up there is.

Without an account, nothing personal is collected

Your term — courses, dates, ticks, study blocks, life entries and anything you typed — is written into this browser's own storage. It stays on this device. Marks are a special case. If you record what you scored on something, that stays on this device and is never sent to the server, never part of your account, and never visible to the owner of this site — not by policy but by construction: the code that talks to the server names the fields it sends, and marks are not among them. They travel only if you export the data file yourself.

The one thing that does leave, account or not, is the visit count set out under What is counted below: a random id and a few numbers, never your course material and never anything you typed.

Your outline stays in your browser

When you paste or choose an outline, it is read and turned into dates by a reader built into this page, running on your own device. On this website the file and its text are never uploaded anywhere — not to JohnsonXCorp, not to an AI service, not to anyone. There is no code path on this site that could send them.

One exception, and it is not this website. Syllabus Desk also exists as a page inside claude.ai. There, and only there, pressing Read this outline may send the extracted text to Anthropic's Claude to be turned into dates, under Anthropic's terms rather than this notice. The review screen names which reader ran, either way.

With an account: an email address and your term

If you choose to make one, two things are stored on a server:

  • the email address you signed up with, together with the date you joined and the date you last signed in;
  • your term — the same courses, dates, ticks and blocks, so they can reach your other devices.

Your password is not stored. Only a one-way hash of it is kept, and a hash cannot be turned back into a password by anyone, JohnsonXCorp included. Your email address is visible to JohnsonXCorp, along with when you joined, when you were last seen and how many courses you hold — that, and nothing else. Access to each person's term is enforced row by row inside the database, so no other signed-in person can read yours. Nothing is sold, rented, or shared with anyone for any purpose.

What it is for. The email address exists to sign you in, to let you reset your own password, and to let the operator answer one question honestly: how many people use this. It is used for nothing else.

What is counted

One thing leaves this browser whether or not you have an account: that the page was opened. The first time you arrive, the page invents a random id for itself, stores it here, and sends it at most once an hour together with your platform (iPhone, Windows, and so on), your timezone, how many times this browser has opened the page, whether it has an account, and how many courses it holds — a number, not the courses.

What is never sent: your name, your email address if you have no account, any course title, any date, any grade, any note, any file, or anything you typed. The id is random and belongs to this browser — it points at nothing and nobody, and clearing your browser data ends it. The table it lands in cannot be read back through this page by anyone, the operator included; the numbers reach him as a daily total. Settings › Counting › Don't count it stops it on this device immediately.

Where it is stored

Accounts and stored terms sit in a Postgres database run by Supabase on Amazon Web Services in the ca‑central‑1 region — Montréal, Canada. Personal information is therefore stored in Canada. Requests to it are routed over Cloudflare's network on the way, so a connection may terminate at an edge server outside Canada in transit; the traffic is encrypted and nothing is stored there. This page itself is served by GitHub Pages, which — like any web host — records the IP address of every visitor in its own server logs, outside the operator's control.

How long it is kept

  • Your term in this browser — until you clear it. Nobody else can reach it.
  • Account and stored term — while the account exists. Gone the moment you press Delete my account. An account with no sign-in for 24 months is deleted along with everything in it.
  • Visit counts — 24 months, then deleted. They are never linked to an account.

3 · Academic accuracy

This is the part that matters most, so it is said plainly. Syllabus Desk is not a source of truth. An outline can be ambiguous, a table can be read the wrong way round, and a date can be revised in class and never appear in any document at all.

What the page does about that: every date the reader proposes is shown to you for confirmation before anything is saved, anything the outline left blank is flagged rather than invented, and a date taken from an unusual place says where it came from. What you must do about it: check anything that matters against your course page or your instructor, and never sit an examination on this page's word alone.
