Syllabus Desk · a JohnsonXCorp project

Your whole term, on one page.

Feed it your course outlines. It pulls out every exam, quiz, report and deadline, lays them out week by week, and hands you a file your phone's calendar understands.

The problem

The dates exist. Nobody can see them.

Every course hands you a PDF with the schedule buried in a table on page four, in a different layout from the last one. Five courses means five documents, five formats, and no single place that says what is due this week.

So most people read the outline once in September and never again — and find out about the midterm when someone mentions it in the hallway.

Worse: the outline is not the whole story. Instructors post quizzes, lessons and gated activities on the course site that the outline never mentions.

What this does

One pass, then it is just there.

You add each course once. Claude reads the outline and proposes the dated items — and then you confirm every one before anything is saved. Nothing is guessed: if the outline never gave a date, it comes back flagged, not invented.

After that you get your term three ways: this week and next for what is actually coming, a full-term calendar for the shape of it, and course by course for the weightings and the rules that quietly cost marks.

Then export it, so the reminders reach you when you are not looking at this page.

Step 01

Add a course

Press Add course. Drop in the PDF or Word file your instructor posted, or paste the text straight from it. The file is read on your own device — it is not uploaded anywhere.

If you only paste part of it, paste the schedule table and the grading breakdown. Those two carry almost every date.

Step 02

Check what it found

Everything lands in an editable table before it is saved. Fix a title, correct a date, change a weight, delete a row that is not really graded.

Rows marked no date are the ones the outline left blank — usually final exams. Fill them in when your school publishes them, or leave them flagged so you remember they are open.

Step 03

Do the rest, then set your study blocks

Repeat for each course. On the Courses tab, each one gets a weekly study block you can move to whatever evening suits you — and extra prep is added automatically five, three and one days before every exam.

Step 04

Send it to your phone

On Export, take the .csv for Google Calendar or the .ics for Apple Calendar and everything else. Import it into a separate calendar, not your main one — if it comes out wrong you delete one calendar instead of forty events.

The export is a snapshot. Change a date here afterwards and your calendar will not know, so re-export or fix that one event by hand.

What it will not do

  • It will sometimes get a date wrong. It reads outlines with an AI model, and outlines are messy. This is a planning aid, not a source of truth — your course page and your instructor are. Check anything that matters.
  • It only knows what you give it. If your instructor posts a quiz on the course site that never appears in the outline, it will not be here. Worth a look at your course page every couple of weeks.
  • Without an account, your term is saved in this browser only. Not on a server, not visible to anyone else — but also not on your phone, and gone if you clear site data. Export data on the Export tab is your backup. With an account, your email and your term are stored on a server run by JohnsonXCorp so they can follow you between devices, and your email is visible to the site owner. Your password is not — only a one-way hash of it is kept, which nobody can read back. Accounts are optional and the Account tab can delete what is stored.
  • Reading an outline sends its text to Claude. That one step, and only that step, leaves your device. Everything else — the file itself, your schedule, your edits — stays local.

Your account

You do not need an account to use this

Everything works without one, exactly as it does now — your term lives in this browser and never leaves it. An account does one job: it lets the same term open on your phone, your laptop and a library computer, and survives clearing your browser data.

What signing up means in plain words. Your email address and your term — courses, dates, ticks and study blocks — are stored on a server run by JohnsonXCorp, and your email is visible to the owner of this site. Your password is not: it is stored as a one-way hash that nobody, the owner included, can read or recover. You can delete everything from this page at any time.

How this page is built

One source, two outputs, so they cannot drift: body.html plus build.py writes the artifact body and the standalone index.html together. The JohnsonXCorp mark is injected from jxc-logo.svg at build time and the build fails if it is missing. Never edit the outputs directly.

Design tokens are the UNI Made EZ system: teal #0E7C86, lime #D9F56B, coral #FF7A59, Inter Tight over Inter with JetBrains Mono, 2px corners, inverted footer.

What this sign-in is and is not

The username and password are stored as a SHA-256 hash in OWNER_HASH, so the password itself is not sitting in the source in readable form. That is better than plain text, and it is still not security: the check runs in your browser, on a page anyone can read and edit, so a determined person gets past it. Treat it as a lock on a glass door.

Which means: treat everything shipped in this file — including the schedule behind Load my schedule — as public, because on a public web address it is.

To change the credentials: compute sha256("username:password"), replace OWNER_HASH in src/body.html, rebuild and republish.

If you want a schedule that is genuinely private, keep it out of the build and bring it in through Import on the Export tab from a JSON file you hold yourself.

Your course material stays yours

Outlines you paste or upload are read in your browser. The file itself is never uploaded.

Without an account, your schedule is stored only in this browser's local storage — not on a server, not visible to anyone else, and not visible to JohnsonXCorp.

With an account, two things are stored on a server so they can reach your other devices: the email address you signed up with, and your term — courses, dates, ticks and study blocks. Your email address is visible to the owner of this site, along with when you joined and when you last signed in. Your password is never visible to anyone, the owner included: only a one-way hash of it is kept, and a hash cannot be turned back into a password. Access is enforced per row in the database, so no other signed-in person can read your term. Accounts are entirely optional, nothing about them is sold or shared, and Delete my saved term on the Account tab removes what is stored whenever you want.

One exception, stated plainly: when you press Read this outline, the extracted text of that outline is sent to Anthropic's Claude so it can be turned into dates, and it is processed under Anthropic's terms rather than this notice. Nothing else on this page is sent anywhere. If you would rather not, build your term by hand instead — every field is editable.
